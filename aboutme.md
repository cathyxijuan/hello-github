## Research summary

I am a quantitative psychologist. My research asks how we can measure
psychological constructs well, and how we can tell whether our statistical
models fit the data. My work falls into four connected areas.

### 1. Better questionnaire formats

Most psychological scales use the Likert format ("strongly disagree" to
"strongly agree"), often with a mix of positively and reverse-worded items. My
work shows that reverse-worded items can distort a scale's factor structure,
for example in the Need for Cognition Scale. I have developed and tested the
**Expanded format**, in which each response option is written out as a full
sentence. Across studies of the Big Five Inventory (BFI and BFI-2) and the
Rosenberg Self-Esteem Scale, the Expanded format gives cleaner factor
structures and better psychometric properties than the Likert format.

### 2. Model fit in structural equation modelling (SEM)

Researchers rely on fit indexes such as RMSEA and CFI to decide whether an SEM
model is adequate. I study how these indexes behave and how to improve them:

- bootstrapping confidence intervals for fit indexes
- showing how missing data distorts RMSEA and CFI under full-information
  maximum likelihood (FIML), and proposing new computations that correct this
- evaluating the fit of the structural (not just the measurement) part of a
  model
- explaining model identification clearly, from its conceptual foundations to
  CFA identification rules

### 3. Making statistical ideas clear

I also write methodological papers that help researchers use statistics
correctly. Topics include how to think clearly about the central limit
theorem, the relationship between nonnormality and moderated multiple
regression, and how to generate missing data for simulation studies.

### 4. AI psychometrics

My newest line of work brings psychometrics and artificial intelligence
together. It includes:

- designing AI agents with personalities using a psychometric approach
- using AI for **automatic item generation** to help develop psychological
  scales, and testing whether this really works
